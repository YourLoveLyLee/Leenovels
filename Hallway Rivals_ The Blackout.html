廊下夫婦

Hallway Rivals

The Blackout

Akane

The apartment was small enough that Akane could hear Hiro's phone from the other side of it. A man was explaining how to fold a fitted sheet. Hiro had never folded a fitted sheet in his life.

He sat on the floor with his back against the sofa, thumb moving up the screen every few seconds. The dishes were done. She had lost to him at rock-paper-scissors anyway. He'd picked rock.

Akane walked over and lay down across his lap, head on his thigh, feet hanging off the sofa arm. He lifted the phone a little to make room and kept scrolling.

Akane“Your legs are bony.”

Hiro“Then use the cushion.”

Akane“The cushion's over there. I'd have to move.”

Hiro“You moved here.”

Akane“That was a different kind of moving.”

She stared at the ceiling. There was a water stain in one corner shaped like a rabbit. She had decided it was a rabbit three days ago. She hadn't planned to say the next thing. It was just there, like a sneeze.

Akane“Hirokawa. Are we dating?”

The phone stopped. His thumb stopped. She kept her eyes on the rabbit and counted two seconds.

Hiro“What?”

Akane“It's a question.”

Hiro“Why would we be dating?”

Akane“I didn't say we are. People keep asking me. Natsuki. Tachibana. Shirakawa asked me in the stairwell and laughed when I said no, and I don't know why he laughed. And everyone went quiet today when I hugged you. Nobody goes quiet when I hug Aizawa.”

Hiro“You don't hug Aizawa.”

Akane“That's my point.”

She waited for the joke. There was always a joke. He'd say dream on and she'd tell him to perish with dignity, and that would be that.

Hiro“I don't know. Maybe they just think that way. People see two people hanging out and decide it means something.”

Hiro“They saw you leaning on me. It's different.”

Akane“Is it?”

Hiro“It's what I'd say if someone asked.”

His voice was exactly the same as always. That was the problem. She had waited for it to change, and it hadn't, and she realized she'd been waiting.

She put both hands over her face.

Why did I say that out loud. It was a question. People ask questions.

She had started with the socks, as a joke, to prove how unsuited he was. He leaves them in different rooms. Then the rock every single time. Then the bird droppings.

He puts his left hand in his pocket first. He taps the desk twice before he answers something he knows. He doesn't like melon bread, but he eats it if I'm the one who bought it. His shower takes eleven minutes.

Why do I know the shower takes eleven minutes.

Huh.

Huh?

HUH?!

Her whole body went stiff against his leg.

Hiro“Yukishiro?”

Akane“Huh.”

Hiro“You said ‘huh.’”

Akane“That was breathing.”

Hiro“It had a shape.”

Akane“It was a very shaped breath. I'm resting my eyes.”

It's an environmental fact. It's like knowing where the stairs are. But I know it better than the stairs.

And he knows mine, doesn't he. He knew which earring I'd lost before I did.

We're not dating. We are so clearly not dating. I'd know.

Wouldn't I?

After a while his hand came down on her hair, lightly, the way you'd steady something that might roll off a table.

Akane“Don't touch my hair.”

Hiro“Then I'll move.”

Akane“Don't move either.”

Hiro“Okay.”

Hiro

After dinner they were back on the couch. The egg had ended up on everything. Hiro had a video playing about a guy trying to break a game where animatronic mascots hunt you through a pizza place. Akane sat beside him under a blanket, supposedly reading.

Akane“That's not how a vent works.”

Hiro“It's a game.”

Akane“Even in a game. You've been watching this for ten minutes, by the way.”

Hiro“You told me to turn it up.”

On screen the rabbit jumped out of a doorway. Akane flinched with her whole shoulder and said “ha” a beat too late. Hiro let it go.

Then the lights went out.

It came with a small electric thunk. The screen died. The fridge stopped humming.

Hiro“Huh. Power.”

He hadn't finished the word when something hit him in the chest. Akane had crossed the couch in one move, blanket half over her head, and landed in his lap with both arms locked around his neck.

Hiro“Yukishiro. It's just a blackout.”

Akane“Don't move. Don't you dare move. Don't go anywhere.”

She was shaking, small and constant, like a phone on vibrate. Rain had started against the window and every drop made her tighten. His apartment had one window across the room and a thin gray rectangle of streetlight that stopped four feet from the couch.

Akane“Look at me.”

Hiro“I can't see you. It's dark.”

Akane“Then look where I am. Please don't look away.”

He looked at the pale edge of her hair against his shoulder.

Akane“It's too dark. Your place is a cave.”

Hiro“You're scared of the dark.”

Akane“I'm not.”

Hiro“You're shaking.”

Akane“It's cold! It's the rain! There's a difference between a stupid thing and the dark, Hirokawa, and you're being, I can't, I don't want to…”

She stopped. He had never heard her like this. In five months she had thrown a shoe at him and once tried to push him into a pond, and she had never made a noise like that. He had a hundred sarcastic things ready and none of them belonged here. So he put one arm flat across her back and one hand behind her head, the way you'd hold a glass steady for someone.

Hiro“You don't have to say it. I'm here. I'm not going anywhere.”

Akane“Promise.”

Hiro“I couldn't move if I wanted to.”

Akane“That's not funny.”

Hiro“It's not meant to be.”

Nobody spoke after that. The rain kept on. Her breathing slowed by degrees, and he counted it without meaning to. A minute, maybe more.

Then she lifted her head.

Her eyes caught the thin light, grey and wet, pointed straight at him. Her hands slid up from his shirt to his collar and held.

She kissed him.

It was short. Her mouth was a little cold and a little unsteady, and it lasted maybe two seconds. His brain stopped on a single word and it wasn't even a good one. She pulled back an inch, then dropped her head onto his shoulder exactly where it had been, and pushed her forehead into his collarbone.

Akane“You're a dummy. You're such a dummy, Hirokawa. You don't know anything.”

Her voice cracked on the last word. She hit his chest once, not hard, and left her fist there.

Akane“I was so scared, and you didn't even know. Five months, and you just sit there, and you eat melon bread when I buy it, and you let me. You let me do every stupid thing.”

She was crying, quietly. The dampness had soaked through his shirt before he noticed. He opened his mouth and nothing came out. He had a whole catalog of responses for Akane, built up over five months of daily use, and he went through them one at a time and every single one was wrong.

Hiro“Yukishiro.”

Akane“Don't. Don't say anything yet.”

Hiro“Okay.”

Akane“And don't look at me.”

Hiro“You told me to look at you.”

Akane“I know what I said!”

He closed his mouth and held on. For the first time in five months, he had nothing to say back.

Later

The lights came back all at once. The fridge shuddered, the lamp flickered, and the man in the vent went on screaming about a rabbit.

Akane lifted her head. She looked at the lamp, at Hiro, at the dark patch on his collar, and at her own hand still gripping it. Her face went blank, then pink from the neck up, then settled into the stiff formal look she used on teachers.

Akane“Remove your hands.”

Hiro“They're not on you. You're on me.”

Akane“Then remove yourself.”

She got off his lap, folded the blanket twice, and picked it up again.

Akane“Nothing happened. There was a blackout, I was in a state, and you will not bring it up.”

Hiro“You kissed me.”

Akane“It was a misjudgment of distance!”

Hiro“For two seconds?”

Akane“It was a prolonged misjudgment!”

She turned and walked into his bedroom. He had time to say “That's my room” before the door slammed hard enough to rattle the picture in the hall. Then came the click of the lock.

Hiro“Yukishiro. That's my bed.”

Akane“Property rights transfer when someone locks a door!”

Hiro“That's not a law.”

Akane“It is in this house! You have a couch! Figure it out! And don't stand at the door!”

He wasn't at the door. He was on the couch, six feet away. He didn't point it out.

He turned off the lights one by one and lay down with his feet over the armrest. There was a stain in the corner of the ceiling shaped like a rabbit. He hadn't noticed that before. From the bedroom came a rustle, a thump like a pillow thrown at a wall, and a muffled groan.

He touched his mouth with two fingers, then put his hand down on his chest.

Hiro“A prolonged misjudgment,” he said, to the ceiling.

Nobody answered. His heart was going far too fast for someone trying to sleep.
